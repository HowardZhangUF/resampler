There is no single universally accepted benchmark suite specifically for particle-filter resamplers. Instead, researchers normally use two types of tests:

Resampler-only benchmarks: Test whether resampling preserves the original probability distribution and runs efficiently.

Particle-filter benchmarks: Replace the resampler inside a fixed particle filter and measure tracking accuracy, posterior uncertainty, and computational cost.

For your goal of developing a closed-form, differentiable resampler, I would use both. The classical comparison by Douc, Cappé, and Moulines and the differentiable optimal-transport work by Corenflos et al. are good methodological starting points.

Best OT reference
TensorFlow implementation connected to the ICML 2021 differentiable optimal-transport PF. Includes ensemble-transform and covariance-corrected variants. Very relevant for comparing your analytic method against iterative transport.

JAX code with a particularly useful experiment comparing PF likelihood gradients against exact Kalman-filter gradients. Also demonstrates stop-gradient resampling.

PyTorch framework with a standard and soft-resampling particle filter. Useful when you want to test end-to-end differentiable models without implementing the complete filter framework yourself.

My recommended starting point is to reproduce the gradient experiment from Cuthbert and the classical resampling comparison from Stone Soup. Those provide two clear, independently testable references. After that, add FilterFlow's transport baseline and your own eigen-resampler.

If your proposed resampler is both faster and provides accurate gradients without damaging the posterior distribution, you will have a stronger contribution than simply showing that it produces a smaller covariance matrix.
